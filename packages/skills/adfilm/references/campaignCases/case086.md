# Is Cadbury's Drumming Gorilla the Best Ad of All Time? (Cadbury · 2015)

## Insight
This entry refers to the 2015 Guardian article and likely a re-run or anniversary replay of the original 2007 Gorilla spot. The cultural reassessment of the Gorilla campaign — years after its original airing — demonstrates the longevity of the P11+P16+P07 mechanic.

## Mechanic
Anniversary or cultural reassessment of the original "Gorilla" spot (2007). The Guardian article re-examined the campaign's impact eight years later, often coinciding with re-airings or retrospective media coverage. The card documents the meta-cultural life of the original spot.

## Why it worked
Great absurdist work with genuine emotional resonance ages better than trend-dependent work. The Gorilla remained legible and pleasurable eight years later because its core mechanism — build tension, release through joy — is structural, not referential. It didn't depend on a meme, a news peg, or a cultural moment that would expire.

## Steal
When evaluating long-term creative value: ask whether the spot depends on contextual relevance (a moment, a trend) or structural mechanism (tension-release, emotional arc). Structural work ages; contextual work expires.
